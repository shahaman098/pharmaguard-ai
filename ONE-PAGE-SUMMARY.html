The problem

Adverse drug events are a leading preventable cause of emergency visits. Polypharmacy — five or more medicines across multiple prescribers — is routine for older adults. The highest-risk gaps are ordinary: warfarin plus an NSAID, a statin plus a strong CYP3A4 inhibitor, calcium binding ciprofloxacin in the gut, or levothyroxine and iron taken together. Most tools stop at a generic warning. They do not resolve the timing, and they do not give the patient or clinician a next action.

The solution

  1. Read the regimen from a label photo. LSTM-based browser OCR proposes medicines and doses; the user confirms. Images never leave the device.
  2. Screen deterministically. A 52-drug, 77-rule demo knowledge base covers CYP450, renal clearance, pharmacodynamic synergy, QT, bleeding, hyperkalemia, and GI chelation.
  3. Fail closed. Medicines outside the knowledge base are marked UNVERIFIED; the app refuses to call the regimen safe and shows coverage explicitly.
  4. Resolve timing with a real solver. A backtracking constraint search enforces fasting windows, meals, bedtime, BID intervals, and 3–4 hour chelation spacing. Unresolved conflicts are reported.
  5. Hand off. One-click SBAR draft for pharmacist review, plain-language patient advice, and a .ics calendar export with dose alarms.

Architecture

Label photo → browser OCR → human confirmation → /api/audit │ deterministic interaction engine (aliases, rule graph, coverage, risk index) │ /api/reschedule → backtracking constraint solver │ daily timeline · weekly/PRN · unresolved report · .ics │ /api/sbar → pharmacist-review SBAR draft

Design principle: AI for perception, deterministic rules for safety, deterministic search for scheduling. No generative model makes a clinical call.

Verification

  • 35/35 tests pass with python3 -m unittest discover tests -v.
  • 20+ high-yield interaction pairs, alias regressions, unknown-drug fail-safe behavior, SBAR structure.
  • Chelation spacing, fasting/meal/bedtime placement, weekly/PRN handling, determinism, day-window bounds.
  • Engineering verification on a curated demo set — not a clinical validation study.

Impact & feasibility

  • Patient: a safe daily schedule and plain-language explanation, exported to their calendar.
  • Caregiver: the whole regimen and the exact questions to ask.
  • Clinician: a structured SBAR draft with verified interactions, unverified medicines, and monitoring recommendations.
  • Deployable: no database, accounts, or stored patient data; OCR runs in the browser.

Why it can win

A complete, working loop with a defensible safety architecture: multimodal intake → deterministic interaction detection → real constraint-based resolution → patient and clinician artifacts. Every claim in the submission is reproducible from the repository, and the app is honest about what it cannot verify.
